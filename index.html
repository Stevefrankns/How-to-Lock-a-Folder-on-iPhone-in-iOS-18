How to Lock a Folder on iPhone in iOS 18

iOS 18 gives iPhone users stronger privacy controls, including the ability to lock many apps with Face ID, Touch ID, or the device passcode. But Apple still does not provide a universal command that adds a separate password directly to one ordinary folder in the Files app. The best method depends on what the “folder” contains.

What “Lock a Folder” Means on an iPhone

On a computer, “lock a folder” often means putting a separate password on a directory. iPhone works differently. iOS protects data through app-level access controls, device encryption, sandboxing, and app-specific privacy features. That is why the right answer changes depending on whether your files live in Files, Notes, Photos, iCloud Drive, or a third-party app.

For the standard Files app, iOS 18 does not expose a command such as Set Folder Password for one folder. You can, however, reduce casual access by locking the app itself. That protects entry to the app on that iPhone, but it does not turn one iCloud Drive folder into an independently encrypted container with its own password.

This distinction matters because searches such as “secure folder iPhone,” “can I lock a folder on iPhone,” and “how to lock Files app on iPhone” describe related but different needs. The safest approach is to match the method to the data instead of assuming every privacy feature provides the same protection.

Method 1 · iOS 18 app lock

How to Lock Access to the Files App in iOS 18

iOS 18 introduced a built-in way to lock supported apps with Face ID, Touch ID, or the device passcode. Apple explains that a locked app requires authentication to open and that information inside a locked app is kept out of places such as notification previews, search, and Siri suggestions.

  1. Go to the Home Screen and locate the Files app.
  2. Touch and hold the app icon until the Quick Actions menu appears.
  3. If available, choose Require Face ID, Require Touch ID, or Require Passcode.
  4. Authenticate and confirm the lock.
  5. Quit Files after use. When a supported app is locked, iOS requires authentication again when you reopen it.
Important limitation

Locking Files protects entry to the app on that iPhone. It does not assign a new password to one folder, and Apple notes that an app’s locked or hidden status does not sync through iCloud. If the same iCloud Drive files are available on another signed-in device, protect that device too.

For Apple’s current instructions, see Lock or hide an app on iPhone.

Method 2 · Notes

How to Lock Private Notes on iPhone

If your “folder” is really a collection of text, scans, lists, or private information, Notes is one of Apple’s strongest built-in options. You can lock supported individual notes with the device passcode or a custom Notes password and use Face ID or Touch ID for convenience.

  1. Open Notes and open the note you want to protect.
  2. Tap the More or Note Actions button.
  3. Tap Lock.
  4. Authenticate using the method configured for that Notes account.

Can you lock an entire Notes folder?

No. Apple’s lock feature applies to individual supported notes, not to a Notes folder as a single locked container. Apple also documents important limits: some notes cannot be locked, including notes with certain attachment types, tags, or unsupported account configurations. Notes synced through some third-party services may not support locking.

If you use a custom Notes password, record it safely. Apple states that resetting a forgotten custom Notes password does not unlock notes that were already protected with the old password.

See Apple’s current Notes locking guidance.

Method 3 · Photos

Use the Locked Hidden Collection for Private Photos and Videos

For photos and videos, do not treat a normal custom album as a secure folder. Instead, move sensitive items to the Hidden collection. Apple locks the Hidden and Recently Deleted collections by default on supported iOS versions, and you can unlock them with Face ID, Touch ID, or the device passcode.

  • In Photos, select the photo or video and choose Hide.
  • Open Collections and find Hidden under Utilities.
  • Authenticate to view the Hidden collection.
  • For extra discretion, you can turn off Show Hidden Album in Photos settings.

Hidden is designed specifically for media. It is not a replacement for a general-purpose protected folder containing PDFs, ZIP files, spreadsheets, or mixed documents.

See Apple’s Photos privacy instructions.

iOS 18 feature

The Hidden Apps Folder Is for Apps, Not Files

One reason iOS 18 searches can be confusing is Apple’s new Hidden folder in App Library. This folder can hold eligible hidden apps and requires authentication to open. It does not hold arbitrary documents from the Files app.

Apple also states that apps that come installed with iPhone cannot be hidden; only eligible downloaded apps can be moved into the Hidden Apps folder. So “hide an app” and “lock a Files folder” are separate features.

If your goal is simply to keep someone using your iPhone from opening a particular app, iOS 18’s app lock is often enough. If your goal is to create a separate protected container for selected documents, use a solution designed around a private vault instead.
